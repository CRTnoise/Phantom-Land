// the idea

Most chat apps are a café.
This is a private line.

A café is convenient, but you're in someone else's building — the owner can listen, lock the doors, or hand over the tapes. Phantom sets up a direct line between your devices. No building. No owner. No tapes.

01 — DISCOVER
Share a code
You create a room and get a code. Send it as text, a link, or a QR scan. No accounts, no signup — for you or anyone you invite.
02 — CONNECT
Peers find peers
Whoever has the code locates you through a distributed network and connects straight to your machine. There's no central server relaying anything.
03 — TALK
End to end
Every message, file, and call is encrypted on your device before it leaves. If the project vanished tomorrow, your conversations keep working.

// engineering

Built honestly.

The hard part wasn't the chat — it was the plumbing nobody sees. And the limitations are stated plainly, because trust is the whole point.

⬡ How it's made

  • Peer discovery over the Hyperswarm DHT — the same family as BitTorrent's.
  • UDP holepunching to connect machines behind home routers, with no relay in the middle.
  • End-to-end encryption with X25519 + XSalsa20-Poly1305 (TweetNaCl primitives).
  • Voice over WebRTC across those direct peer connections.
  • Local SQLite storage — your data never leaves unencrypted.

⬡ Honest limits

  • Not open source, and not yet independently security-audited.
  • "No server" has an asterisk: the public DHT has bootstrap nodes you need to first enter the network.
  • Symmetric NAT / carrier-grade NAT is the genuine weak spot of any pure-P2P design.
  • Great against Big Tech and data harvesting — not built to defeat state-level adversaries.
